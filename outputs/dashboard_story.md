# Executive Dashboard Story
### Retail Business Performance | January 2024 – December 2025

---

## Executive Summary

Over the two-year period from January 2024 to December 2025, the retail business generated **₹21.7 Cr in total revenue** and **₹3.33 Cr in net profit**, sustaining an overall profit margin of **15.3%** across 4,200 orders. The business is broadly healthy — profitable across all regions and customer segments — but carries three clear risks that leadership must address: a structurally low-margin Furniture category, an unchecked discounting practice that is actively producing losses at the 30–35% discount tier, and a disproportionately high return rate in Furniture that compounds its already-thin margins.

The dashboard tells a story of a business with a strong Technology core, balanced customer segments, and room to significantly improve profitability by tightening discount policies and addressing Furniture's unit economics.

---

## What Is Performing Well

**Technology is the profit engine of the business.** It accounts for ₹15.4 Cr in sales (70.9% of total revenue) and ₹2.80 Cr in profit (84.2% of total profit) at an 18.2% margin — well above the company average. Within Technology, all four sub-categories — Copiers, Phones, Accessories, and Machines — maintain margins between 18–19%, indicating a consistent and scalable profit profile. This is the category the business should protect and grow aggressively.

**The South region leads in both revenue and profit**, generating ₹6.47 Cr in sales and ₹1.00 Cr in profit. However, the spread between regions is narrow (South at ₹99.9 L vs West at ₹74.0 L), suggesting a well-distributed business without dangerous geographic concentration.

**All three customer segments are profitable and well-balanced.** Home Office, Consumer, and Corporate each contribute roughly ₹7 Cr in sales and over ₹1 Cr in profit. No segment is structurally underperforming, which is a sign of healthy customer diversification.

**Standard Class shipping — which handles 58% of all orders — is performing at an average of 4.7 days**, which is within acceptable service levels. Same Day and First Class options exist and are being used appropriately for premium customers.

**Organic channel drives the highest volume**, with 1,694 orders generating ₹8.88 Cr in sales and ₹1.34 Cr in profit. This suggests strong brand recognition and word-of-mouth strength — an asset worth protecting.

---

## What Is Underperforming

**Furniture is the most significant underperformer.** With a category-level margin of just 6.9%, Furniture barely breaks even when returns, shipping costs, and overheads are factored in. The two weakest sub-categories — Tables (5.7% margin) and Bookcases (5.7%) — are the worst performers across all 13 sub-categories in the entire portfolio. Yet Furniture accounts for ₹5.16 Cr in sales (24% of revenue), meaning this is not a small problem.

**High discounts are generating losses.** The 64 orders placed at 30–35% discount produced a collective net loss of ₹1.02 L with a -4.4% average margin. Even the 20–30% discount bucket averages only 6.8% margin — barely above Furniture's already-poor baseline. The business is subsidizing customers at the expense of profitability without a clear policy framework.

**Furniture also has the highest return rate at 7.7%**, more than double Technology's 3.0%. Returns erode already-thin margins further and create hidden fulfilment costs (reverse logistics, restocking, possible write-offs). The Home Office segment, which has the highest return rate (5.7%), disproportionately purchases Furniture, creating a compounding risk.

**April is a weak month across both years.** Sales in April 2024 (₹80.1 L) and April 2025 (₹71.9 L) are among the lowest of any month, suggesting a predictable revenue gap that is not being addressed through targeted promotions or campaigns.

---

## What Risks Are Visible

1. **Discount Control Risk:** The absence of a hard cap on discounts at 30–35% is causing active value destruction. If this persists at scale, it will materially erode overall company margins.

2. **Furniture Margin Risk:** As Furniture grows, it dilutes the company's blended margin. If sales mix shifts toward Furniture and away from Technology, the business could see overall profitability decline even with revenue growth.

3. **Return Rate Risk in Furniture + Home Office:** Furniture returns (7.7%) combined with the Home Office segment's elevated return rate (5.7%) suggest quality, expectation, or logistics issues that, unaddressed, will worsen customer lifetime value metrics.

4. **Slow Shipment Risk:** 666 orders (15.9%) fall into the "6+ days" delivery bucket. If these are concentrated in specific corridors or with specific logistics partners, they represent a customer satisfaction risk that is not visible in aggregate NPS or rating data.

5. **Seasonal Revenue Gap:** April consistently underperforms. Without countermeasures, the business accepts this gap as a fixed cost — but it is addressable.

---

## What Opportunities Are Visible

1. **Technology upsell within existing accounts:** All three segments purchase Technology products, but at different rates. Structured cross-sell programs — especially for Corporate and Home Office accounts that already trust the brand — could drive margin-accretive revenue.

2. **Rajasthan, Telangana, and Tamil Nadu are high-potential states** that can absorb more investment. These top-3 states contribute ₹4.3 Cr in combined sales — dedicated regional teams and state-specific campaigns could lift this further.

3. **Email channel is underutilised:** With only 657 orders but a strong ₹5.36 L profit contribution, Email channel ROI is compelling. Expanding the email list and frequency of targeted campaigns could drive incremental profitable revenue.

4. **Referral channel has the highest trust signal** (392 orders, ₹3.34 L profit). A structured referral incentive program could amplify this low-cost, high-quality acquisition channel.

5. **Rationalising Furniture discounts alone could add ₹15–20 L in annual profit** by bringing discount discipline to a category that can least afford generosity.

## Recommended Business Actions

| Priority | Action | Expected Impact |
|---|---|---|
| **High** | Cap all discounts at 25%; require Director approval above 20% | Eliminate ₹1.02 L loss zone; improve blended margin |
| **High** | Review Furniture pricing — raise prices 3–5% or reduce COGs | Lift Furniture margin from 6.9% to 9–10% |
| **High** | Investigate Furniture return root causes; improve QC and packaging | Reduce 7.7% return rate to industry benchmark of <4% |
| **Medium** | Invest in Rajasthan, Telangana, TN regional teams | Potential 15–20% uplift in top-state revenue |
| **Medium** | Scale Email marketing — expand list, automate drip campaigns | Capture ₹1–2 Cr additional revenue at high margin |
| **Medium** | SLA audit on 666 slow shipments; renegotiate slow logistics lanes | Improve CSAT and reduce churn from delivery dissatisfaction |
| **Low** | Run April promotional campaigns 2 weeks in advance | Smooth seasonal revenue dip |
| **Low** | Build Referral incentive program | Double referral orders at minimal acquisition cost |

## Limitations of the Dashboard

- **No customer-level lifetime value (LTV) data:** The dataset contains orders but not customer tenure or repeat purchase rates. LTV-based segmentation would significantly enhance targeting decisions.
- **No cost-of-goods breakdown:** Profit = Sales − Profit is a proxy for cost, but true cost structure (product COGs vs overheads vs shipping costs) is not separable in this dataset.
- **Return reasons not captured:** The `return_flag` indicates a return occurred but provides no information on *why* — making root cause analysis of returns impossible from this data alone.
- **Campaign attribution is incomplete:** 24 orders have no campaign channel assigned. This may understate the effectiveness of specific channels.
- **Customer ratings have 32 null values:** Minor gap, but rating-based segmentation is slightly imprecise.
- **State-level data is present but city-level granularity varies** — some states are represented by many cities, others by few, making city-level decisions inconsistent.

---

## Suggested Next Analysis

1. **Customer Cohort Analysis:** Analyse repeat purchase rates by segment and acquisition channel to identify which cohorts deliver the best LTV.
2. **Return Root Cause Study:** Conduct a qualitative survey of returned Furniture orders to identify the top 3 fixable causes.
3. **Discount Elasticity Modelling:** Test whether reducing discounts from 20–25% to 15–20% reduces volume — or whether demand is inelastic, meaning full margin can be captured.
4. **State-Level Saturation Analysis:** Map sales per capita (or per business) by state to identify where the addressable market is largest relative to current penetration.
5. **Shipping Partner Performance Audit:** Cross-reference the 666 "slow" shipments with carrier/logistics partner data to identify underperforming lanes and renegotiate SLAs.
6. **Seasonal Campaign A/B Testing:** Run differentiated April campaigns across channels and measure lift to determine the optimal promotional mix for soft months.
